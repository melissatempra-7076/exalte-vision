The idea

The "Les Mills" of hotel wellness

Les Mills doesn't run gyms. It creates programs like BodyPump, releases fresh versions every quarter, certifies instructors at other people's gyms and charges a licence fee. exalte could do the same for hospitality: licensed seasonal wellness programs that hotels deliver with their own trained staff, under the exalte brand, with your house of practitioners as the creators.

1

Create

Roster practitioners co-create each season: in-room rituals, group sessions, an 80/20 menu and a residency format.

2

Release

Four exalte Seasons a year, from Winter "Rest" to Spring "Rise". Each launch is a PR moment.

3

License

Hotels pay a monthly fee per property to run the exalte program.

4

Certify

Hotel spa and wellness staff train as certified exalte Guides, refreshed each season.

5

Deliver

Hotel staff run the everyday rituals. exalte practitioners lead the premium residencies and events.

6

Reward

Practitioners earn a royalty each time a hotel delivers their ritual, like a musician whose song is played.

A proven model

It started with one gym in Auckland

In 1968 Les Mills opened a small gym in Auckland. His son Phillip and daughter-in-law Jackie created BODYPUMP in 1990, and in 1997 founded Les Mills International to license their programs to gyms they didn't own. A Canberra gym owner helped shape the licensing model that took it worldwide.

The lessons: it took years and real persistence before licensing paid off, and the business thrived when it stayed focused on programs rather than side ventures. Les Mills serves gyms. We haven't found anyone doing the same for hotels.

Why hotels want it

  • Rates and revenuePremium wellness packages and rooms. WITT reports wellness-designated hotels achieve about 20% higher average daily rates
  • Quiet seasonsWinter and shoulder programs give guests a reason to book
  • Dining and spa spendSeasonal menus, elixirs and signature treatments
  • Conference businessWellness add-ons that help win corporate events
  • Marketing and loyaltyA launch every quarter and stays guests talk about

Why not build it themselves? They would need to create content, vet practitioners, refresh programs every season, train staff and market it. Licensing is faster and better, the same reason gyms license Les Mills.

What's different

A gap in the market

Hotel wellness certification exists, but it assesses buildings, not programs. Hotel wellness deals are mostly digital (Peloton, Calm) or one brand at one property (Fluidform, KIC, Aqua Sculpt). We haven't found a licensed, seasonal, staff-delivered program for hotels that rewards its creators with royalties. Worth confirming with a proper market and trademark check.

The path

Start small, then scale

Pilot

Build Winter "Rest" and license it to 3 properties in one boutique group, through a Reymond relationship.

Prove

Three months measuring package sales, guest feedback, staff confidence and renewals.

Partner

Take the results to a major group such as IHG, Accor or Hyatt.

Expand

New Zealand and the Pacific, then Asia, then the Northern Hemisphere.

Most wellness brands grow one city at a time. exalte could grow one hotel group at a time, worldwide.
